Martial arts pricing comes down to two decisions: what to charge each month, and whether to ask families to commit for a term. This guide gives you a way to set tuition from your own costs, shows what ten real programs publish as their prices, and then sets out what federal law and six states say about membership contracts. If you are still setting up the school, our [martial arts software](/martial-arts-software/) page shows how BeltManager handles students, belts and attendance once the doors are open.

Every price and every rule below is linked in the sources at the end, with the date we checked it. This is general information, not legal advice. Contract rules differ by state and some of them turn on fine distinctions, so have an attorney in your state read your agreement before you use it.

Three things are worth noticing.

- **Public programs price by session or with a resident rate.** They are often subsidized and use public buildings, so a commercial school cannot match them on price and does not need to. Know what the recreation department near you charges, because parents do.
- **The commercial examples include both models.** Two say month to month in plain words, one states a 12-month contract, and one has no contract but asks for 30 days' notice. Each tells the reader the term on the price page.
- **The headline price is not the whole price.** Uniform fees, annual membership fees and belt test fees appear in these examples. Decide which of those you charge and say so up front. Our [belt testing guide](/blog/belt-testing-how-often-what-to-charge/) covers test fees.

One official number gives context for price increases. In the Consumer Price Index for September 2026, the Bureau of Labor Statistics category "Fees for lessons or instructions" rose 2.7 percent over the 12 months to September 2026 [11]. That category covers lessons of all kinds, not martial arts alone.

## How to Set Your Tuition From Your Costs

A price copied from the school down the road carries that school's rent and class sizes, not yours. Build yours from the ground up, then compare.

1. **Add up your monthly costs.** Rent and utilities, instructor pay and payroll taxes (see [what instructors cost](/blog/martial-arts-instructor-pay/)), insurance (see [martial arts insurance](/blog/martial-arts-insurance/)), association fees, software, marketing, loan payments and a reserve for repairs.
2. **Add what you need to take home.** If you leave this out, the school only works while you work for free.
3. **Estimate paying students conservatively.** Use the number you have now, or the number you can realistically reach in the first year, not the number the room holds.
4. **Divide.** Costs plus owner pay, divided by paying students, is your break-even tuition.
5. **Adjust for discounts.** If one family in five pays a reduced rate, the full rate has to be higher than break-even to make up the difference.
6. **Compare with local published prices.** If your number is far above them, look at your costs and class sizes before you look at your price.

*Worked example. Every figure here is made up to show the arithmetic; put in your own.*

| Line | 80 students | 110 students |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly costs (rent, pay, insurance, everything else) | $9,000 | $9,000 |
| Owner pay | $4,000 | $4,000 |
| **Total to cover each month** | **$13,000** | **$13,000** |
| **Break-even tuition per student** | **$162.50** | **$118.18** |

The same school needs $162.50 a month from each of 80 students or $118.18 from each of 110. That gap is why the student count matters more than any other number, and why a price that works for an established school can sink a new one. The [dojo revenue calculator](/dojo-revenue-calculator/) lets you run this with your own figures and extra income such as test fees and camps. If you have not opened yet, start with [what it costs to open a school](/blog/cost-to-open-gym/).

### Ways to structure the price

| Structure | How it works | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| One monthly rate, unlimited classes | Everyone pays the same | Simple to explain and to run | Students who train once a week may feel overcharged |
| Tiers by classes per week | A lower rate for one class a week, a higher one for unlimited | Families watching the budget; beginners | You have to track who attends what |
| Family rate | A set price for a household, or a lower price for each additional member | Schools with many siblings | Decide whether it applies to parents who train too |
| Session or term fee | One price for a fixed block of weeks | Programs in schools and community buildings | Income arrives in lumps; re-enrollment every term |
| Class packs and drop-ins | A price per class or per block of classes | Adults with irregular schedules; visitors | Unpredictable income |
| Paid in full | A discount for paying several months or a year ahead | Cash at the start of the year | Refund rules, and in some states limits on prepayment (see below) |

Whatever you choose, write down what the price includes: how many classes, which programs, the uniform, test fees, and any annual fee.

## Month-to-Month or a Term Agreement?

"Contract" in this business usually means an agreement to pay for a fixed term, often 6 or 12 months. Month to month means the family can stop with short notice. Neither is the honest choice or the greedy one. They trade different things.

| | Month to month | Term agreement |
|---|---|---|
| For the family | Easy to try; easy to leave | A commitment; often a lower monthly rate in return |
| For your income | Less predictable from month to month | Steadier, if you are willing to enforce it |
| For your time | Little paperwork | Agreements to write, sign, store and renew |
| When someone wants out | They give notice and stop | You need a written rule for moving, injury and hardship, and the will to apply it evenly |
| Legal exposure | Lower, though autopay and renewal rules still apply | Higher: several states regulate term contracts directly |

A common middle course is month to month with a notice period, as one of the schools above publishes, or a short first term followed by month to month. If you do use a term, decide in advance what happens when a family moves away, a student is injured, or a parent loses a job, and write it into the agreement.

## What the Law Says About Membership Contracts

More than one set of rules can apply to the same agreement. The summary below quotes the rules as published. It does not tell you which of them covers your school; that depends on your state, how your school is set up and what you sell.

### Federal rules

**There is no federal click-to-cancel rule in force.** The Federal Trade Commission adopted one in 2024 and a federal appeals court vacated it. On February 12, 2026, the Commission published a final rule restoring the earlier text "in the form it existed before the 2024 Rule became effective", and that earlier rule covers only prenotification plans. On March 13, 2026, it published an advance notice asking for comment on whether to amend the rule again, with comments due April 13, 2026 [12]. We found no later proposal when we checked on November 22, 2026.

**Recurring charges sold online are covered by a statute.** The Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act makes it unlawful to charge a consumer through a negative option feature in an internet transaction unless the seller clearly and conspicuously discloses all material terms before getting billing information, obtains "express informed consent" before charging, and "provides simple mechanisms for a consumer to stop recurring charges" [13]. A negative option is an arrangement where the charge continues until the customer says stop, which describes most auto-renewing memberships sold through a website.

**Automatic bank drafts have their own rule.** Under Regulation E, recurring transfers from a consumer's bank account "may be authorized only by a writing signed or similarly authenticated by the consumer", and the business must give the consumer a copy. The consumer can stop a payment by telling their bank "at least three business days before the scheduled date of the transfer". If the amount will differ from the last one, the consumer is entitled to written notice at least 10 days before [14].

**The three-day federal cooling-off right does not cover a sale at your school.** The FTC's Cooling-Off Rule covers certain sales made at a buyer's home, workplace or dormitory, or at a seller's temporary location. It does not cover sales "made after completing negotiations at the seller's permanent place of business" [23]. Any right to cancel after signing at the school comes from state law or from your own agreement.

### Six state examples

Many states regulate "health club", "health studio" or "health spa" contracts. Some of those laws name martial arts and some do not. These six are examples of how much the rules differ. Each row is taken from the statute on the state legislature's own site.

| State and law | Does it name martial arts? | Limits on the contract | Right to cancel after signing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York, General Business Law Article 30 (health club services) | Yes: "the martial arts to include, judo, karate and self-defense", and health clubs include "martial arts and self-defense schools" | Not more than $3,600 a year; no term longer than 36 months | Within three business days of receiving the written contract; also on death, a disability of more than three months, or a move of more than 25 miles | [17] |
| Washington, RCW 19.142 (health studio services) | Yes: the definition of health studio lists "the martial arts" | Written contract; payments over no more than 36 months; dues raised no more than once in a calendar year | By the third business day after signing; a contract of more than one year can be canceled for any reason on 30 days' written notice | [21] |
| Maryland, Commercial Law Title 14, Subtitle 12B (health club services) | It names a "self defense school". It applies where services run longer than 3 months, the seller collects more than 3 months' payment in advance, and the service is for personal, family or household use | No automatic renewal clause unless the buyer accepts a renewal option | Within 3 business days of receiving a copy of the agreement | [20] |
| Florida, sections 501.012 to 501.019 (health studios) | Only in an exemption: a program "offered and used solely for the purpose of dance, aerobic exercise, or martial arts, and which utilizes no physical exercise equipment" may be declared exempt on filing an affidavit with the state | Initial contract of no more than 36 months, then renewable annually; a $25,000 bond unless an exemption applies | Within 3 days, not counting holidays and weekends; also on death or if the buyer becomes physically unable to use a substantial portion of the services; and if the studio goes out of business, or moves more than 5 driving miles without providing a facility of equal quality within 5 driving miles inside 30 days | [19] |
| California, Civil Code sections 1812.80 to 1812.98 (health studio services) | No. The definition covers instruction in "physical culture, body building, exercising, reducing, figure development, or any other such physical skill" | Term of no more than three years; total payments of no more than $4,400 | Until midnight of the fifth business day after the date of the agreement; a contract that does not comply is "void and unenforceable" | [15] |
| Texas, Occupations Code Chapter 702 (Health Spa Act) | No. A health spa is a business selling memberships for "instruction in or the use of facilities for a physical exercise program"; the list of facilities includes "martial arts equipment" | A state operator's certificate is required; contract term of no more than three years | Full refund, due within 30 days, if the member sends written notice by certified mail by midnight of the third business day after the contract date | [18] |

How to read the table:

- **Where a law names martial arts, read the whole statute,** including the bond, the required wording and the exemptions.
- **Where it does not, do not assume you are outside it.** Whether a karate or jiu-jitsu school is a "health studio" or "health spa" under wording like California's or Texas's is a legal question we cannot answer for you. Ask an attorney or the state agency that administers the law.
- **The counting rules differ.** "Business day" is defined differently from state to state, and some laws say how the notice must be sent.
- **Several of these laws dictate wording and type size** for the cancellation notice. An agreement downloaded from another state may be missing it.
- **Your state may not be here.** We opened these six; other states have their own laws.

### Automatic renewal

Some states also regulate automatic renewal separately. California's law, as amended with effect for contracts entered into, amended or extended on or after July 1, 2025, makes it unlawful to charge a consumer's credit or debit card, or account with a third party, for an automatic renewal "without first obtaining the consumer's affirmative consent", requires the renewal terms to be presented clearly before the consumer agrees, requires an acknowledgment the consumer can keep that explains how to cancel, and requires the business to keep proof of consent for at least three years, or one year after the contract ends, whichever is longer [16]. New York's health club law requires a club to accept cancellation of a monthly renewal within three business days after the renewal takes effect, through methods "including, but not limited to, website, electronic mail, telephone, mail, or in person" [17].

### When the student is a child

Most of your students may be minors, and a minor's signature is a weak foundation for a contract. California's Family Code, for example, says that "a contract of a minor may be disaffirmed by the minor before majority or within a reasonable time afterwards" [22]. The practical rule is that the agreement is between the school and a parent or guardian, who signs it and is responsible for payment. The same goes for the liability waiver, which has its own state-by-state rules; see [martial arts insurance, waivers and LLC basics](/blog/martial-arts-insurance/).

## What to Put in Writing

Month to month or term, put the same things on one or two pages and give the family a copy. Your attorney may add wording your state requires.

1. **Who the agreement is between:** the school's legal name and the adult who is responsible for payment.
2. **What is included:** programs, classes per week, uniform, test fees, annual fees.
3. **The price and when it is due,** and what happens if a payment is late.
4. **The term:** month to month, or the start and end date.
5. **Renewal:** whether it renews, for how long and at what price, in plain words near the signature.
6. **How to cancel:** who to tell, how (email should be enough), how much notice, and what is owed after notice.
7. **Any cancellation right your state requires,** in the wording and type size the statute sets.
8. **Early exit for a move, injury or hardship,** if it is a term agreement.
9. **Freezes:** whether a family can pause for a vacation or injury, for how long, and at what cost.
10. **Price changes:** how much notice you will give before raising tuition.
11. **Payment authorization:** if you draft a bank account or charge a card, a signed authorization and a copy for the family [14].

Then follow it the same way for every family. An agreement you enforce for some families and waive for others causes more arguments than no agreement at all.

## Raising Tuition

Costs rise, and a school that never raises tuition ends up cutting something else. Three habits make it easier.

- **Say when you review prices.** Once a year, at the same time, is easier for families to plan around than surprises. Washington's statute goes further for the contracts it covers and limits dues increases to once in a calendar year [21].
- **Give written notice well ahead,** and say what the new rate is and when it starts. Check your own agreement and your state's renewal rules for any minimum notice.
- **Be consistent about existing students.** Either everyone moves to the new rate on the same date or current students keep their rate for a stated period. Decide once and write it down.
